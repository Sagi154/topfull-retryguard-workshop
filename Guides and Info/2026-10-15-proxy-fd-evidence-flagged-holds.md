## Verdict

The indirect evidence supports file-descriptor exhaustion as the usual cause of a long throttle flag, and it supports damage to goodput, P95, rejection, and retries only on a minority of those holds. The two S2 re-runs that saved the proxy log show `accept4: too many open files` within 5 s of the first admin timeout: 429 lines from 189 s on `final_s2_tf_only_run3`, and 568 lines from 50 s on `final_s2_both_burst_run3`. All 10,738 `fetch failed` warnings (49 of the 50 audit-flagged holds plus the two 2026-10-15 re-runs; the 50th, `final_s3_rg_only_normal_run1`, has none) say `timed out`, which is what a full accept queue does to a new connection, and the flags sit on the high-connection mixes (10 of the S2 holds, 39 of the S4A holds, almost all of them catalog-constrained) while every S1 and S4B hold is clean. TopFull-off holds are half of the flags (25 of 50), which fits a Go proxy on port 8090 that every Locust request passes through, including `rg_only`. Against shape-matched clean controls, eleven holds are materially worse after onset (six of the ten flagged S2s, the two logged re-runs, and three S4A-constrained holds): full-window goodput runs about 40–150 req/s below the clean change, or rejection in the next minute runs 0.10–0.40 higher, and `final_s2_tf_only_run2` is the clearest indirect case (goodput 402 → 317 req/s and P95 564 → 1318 ms after 333 s). The counterexamples are as clear as the hits. Three flagged S4A-unconstrained holds keep goodput near 720 req/s and rejection near 0 through 365–545 s of admin timeouts. `s2_burst20_rgtf0` climbs like a clean S2 (goodput 299 → 530, rejection 0.03 → 0.05) through 555 s stale. A 45 s tail blip on `final_s2_rg_only_burst_run2` does not move the rates. Clean S4A-constrained holds with no flag and no admin timeout already fall from about 160 to about 40–50 req/s and about 0.93 rejection, and most flagged S4A-constrained holds land in that same place. Run folders contain no Locust failures CSV and no `RemoteDisconnected` or `connection refused` text, so the client failure mode is inferred from rates. The link from a sustained flag to descriptor exhaustion is strong. The link from that stall to the paper's goodput, P95, rejection, and retry numbers is moderate on S2 and weak on S4A.

## How the times and the rates were aligned

Onset is seconds from the first row of `total.csv` (the Locust stats history) to the earlier of the first `fetch failed` warning in `topfull_throttle_collector.log` and the start of the first stretch longer than 30 s with no fresh threshold or admitted read in `topfull_throttle.csv`. The stretch starts at the last fresh sample. The warning is almost always one poll later, about 5 s. Holds with a `timestamp` column use that UTC stamp directly. Holds without one use the in-memory fit from `experiments/hold_clock.py` (`compute_clock` only; nothing was written into the run folder): a start time and a row spacing of about 1.08–1.11 s, anchored on frontend inbound rate. Throttle logs, proxy logs, and mesh CSVs are already UTC, so the same subtraction puts them on the hold clock. On stamped holds the fit disagrees with the stamp by about 2 s. Four flagged fits have correlation under 0.8, so their onset could be off by tens of seconds: `final_s3_rg_only_normal_run1` (0.11), `final_4a_constrained_both_burst_run2` (0.55), `final_4a_constrained_both_normal_run2_r2` (0.50), and `final_4a_constrained_tf_only_run1_r2` (0.62). The S2 study holds that lack a stamp have correlation 0.92–0.98.

A threshold gap of 5 s next to an admitted gap of several hundred seconds does not mean `/thresholds` kept answering. The collector marks threshold freshness from `rate_config` files when the HTTP call fails, and admitted rate has no file fallback. Those rows still have paired `thresholds fetch failed` and `stats fetch failed` warnings. The warning is the HTTP evidence.

Locust `Fail` is a per-second rate (SLO miss or a non-OK result, as recorded by `metric_collector.py`), not an exception string. The failure count in the table is the sum of `Fail` times the row spacing, so the after-onset count is larger mostly because that window is longer and offered RPS is higher. The comparable figure is `rej`, which is that count divided by offered requests. Typed mesh failures are positive deltas of frontend inbound `5xx` and `resets`, and retries are edge rpr (`Δretry / (Δtotal − Δretry)` on `service_edges.csv`). None of the 83 folders contains a `*fail*` CSV. Searched log text has no `RemoteDisconnected`, `connection refused`, or `ConnectTimeout`. Every admin warning is `timed out`. The two proxy logs add `accept4: too many open files`.

Clean controls are split at the median onset of the flagged holds in that scenario (S2 at 86 s, S3 at 109 s, S4A at 42 s). S1 and S4B have no flags, so they are split at 120 s. S4A controls are matched constrained against constrained and unconstrained against unconstrained, because the clean constrained holds collapse and the clean unconstrained holds do not. A flagged hold is marked **affected** when the stall lasts at least 120 s and the full before/after goodput change is at least 40 req/s worse than the median clean control at the same second, or the 60 s window around onset shows a rejection increase at least 0.10 above those controls together with a goodput shortfall. **confounded** means an S4A-constrained hold whose goodput change stays within that 40 req/s of the clean constrained collapse. **healthy** means goodput keeps climbing and rejection stays near 0 through the stall. **brief** means the stale span is under 120 s. **mild** is the one S2 hold whose full-window shortfall is only 30 req/s.

Their absolute numbers are often poor (goodput falling toward the tens of req/s, rejection toward 0.6–0.95), and that poor level is already the clean constrained result: `final_4a_constrained_both_burst_run1` goes 170 → 51 req/s and rejection 0.27 → 0.92 with zero admin timeouts, and the other clean constrained controls do the same (167 → 44, 143 → 37). Reading a flagged S4A-constrained folder as "the proxy outage destroyed goodput" double-counts the catalog bottleneck.

Offered RPS stays up after onset on the damaged holds as well as the healthy ones. A dead accept path would send Locust RPS and frontend inbound toward zero. What shows up instead is a higher failure fraction with the offered load still near the target, which is the same picture as the logged holds: new connections fail, connections that are already open keep carrying traffic.
